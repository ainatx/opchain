## What actually ran

The application is intentionally small but crosses meaningful boundaries: orders, stock, invoice insertion and tenant/event idempotency share one SQLite write transaction. Ten requests exercise creation, exact retry, conflicting retry, malformed quantity, insufficient inventory, another tenant, valid lookup, tenant-isolated missing result, missing ID and unauthorized identity. Two overlapping deliveries execute through separate SQLite connections and create one invoice.

The prepared target is a read projection, with tenant composite identity and required USD currency. This is separate synthetic input, not an extraction approved through the real-data gate. Backfill stops after one row and persists a cursor; count mismatch blocks readiness. Resume and repeated replay converge to three exact rows. Canonical row hash is `fe6ab0fbceb8d9af1207c52e9057a3ace1d7d0b64a10f8e3e42e2d490ef1a645`. Five read cases preserve exact semantic responses. Missing/extra row injections are detected, null currency is rejected without committing a bad row, and invoice references remain intact.

Source remains primary and byte-identical throughout the rehearsal. Backup restoration matches source SHA-256 `413267ff24e163a08954c84a4cb5ad6597949a316de153053edb626fcb84c5f6`. No destructive schema contract/drop, network write transfer or live route switch occurs.

The local server ran **82 HTTP requests**, then shut down: 40 normal, 40 with a deliberate 30ms delay, and two failing health probes. Normal p95 was **0.638ms**; degraded p95 was **36.477ms**, correctly crossing the predeclared 20ms fixture budget. Both health probes returned 503. Raw samples and configuration are in `consumer/scaling/benchmark.json`. Sequential loopback rate is not service capacity. Monthly production SLO/error-budget consumption and real traffic volume remain unknown.

The monitoring workflow explicitly consumes these results: partial backfill blocks promotion, partial replica health halts rollout, and the controlled latency fault trips its local threshold. These are local rule evaluations against the recorded trace, not installed alerts, real incident notifications or proof of multi-window production monitoring. The postmortem distinguishes logical sequence from production timestamps and identifies deliberate injected causes rather than inventing an outage.
